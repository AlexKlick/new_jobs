# Alex Klick



## Opening

When I built a RAG ingestion pipeline with sub-section-level PDF chunking for a Fortune-200 biopharma client, I learned something that's shaped how I think about retrieval: precision at the chunking layer directly determines whether users trust the answers they get back. That project required processing tens of thousands of regulatory documents, and the difference between retrieving a relevant paragraph versus a vaguely related page was the difference between a system people used and one they ignored. Weaviate sits at the center of this problem for thousands of developers building AI applications. The opportunity to work on infrastructure that makes retrieval systems more reliable, more flexible, and easier to operate is what draws me to these engineering roles.

## Role Alignment

My background aligns with what Weaviate is building in several concrete ways. I've spent the past three years implementing vector databases and reranker systems in production environments, working primarily with pgvector and Chroma while evaluating embedding strategies across OpenAI and open-source models. I've led model-quality evaluations for sentence-transformers rerankers and deployed the selected approaches at scale. My day-to-day work has been in Python and TypeScript, building FastAPI backends that serve LLM-powered features. Beyond the technical stack, I'm comfortable moving from ambiguous business requirements to shipped, observable systems—a pattern that's defined my consulting work and one that seems relevant for a company building developer infrastructure.

## Evidence Highlights

At Credera, I built a vector search and reranker system that powers internal enterprise chat tools used daily by hundreds of consultants. The system indexes tens of thousands of documents, and I led the quality analysis to select the best sentence-transformers model for our reranking layer. More recently, I designed modular agent components for a workflow automation platform that started with ~100 pilot users and is now expanding to 300+ with broader enterprise rollout. That work involved prototyping the architecture, building the initial component set, and partnering closely with design and business analysts to iterate based on user feedback. I've also mentored three junior engineers on improving reliability and performance in agentic workflows—something I'd be glad to continue doing in a team environment.
